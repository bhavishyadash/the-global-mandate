# Session 44: executive consequences

When the fictional reporting directive reaches full implementation, it adds 10 points to the Administration service target (clamped to 100) and incurs a $5 million annual cost. Services move gradually toward their target and influence group approval through existing rules. Effects start the day after implementation; revocation stops the target bonus and future costs while preserving prior service changes and spending. Injunction and rejected override remain without these effects. The existing save format derives the effects from directive status, so already-implemented reporting in older saves acquires the new rules on load. Tests cover the implementation boundary, budget, delayed service and approval response, save/load and revocation. These are invented test assumptions, not claims about procurement policy or executive law.
